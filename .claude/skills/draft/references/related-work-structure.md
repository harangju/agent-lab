# Related Work Structure

Use this for Related Work / Relation to Prior Literature sections, and for intro positioning paragraphs that must contrast with neighbors. Goal: place the paper against the nearest serious alternatives, not inventory a field.

## Sequence

1. Scope the neighborhood.
   - One sentence on which literatures or debates you are joining.
   - Exclude distant citations; depth beats coverage.

2. Nearest neighbors, by claim cluster.
   - Group papers that share a question, mechanism, or method.
   - For each cluster: what they established (shared ground).

3. Hard contrast.
   - Where those lines stop short of your question, setting, or identification.
   - Name the concrete difference (estimand, institution, theory object, scale), not "we are different."

4. Your wedge.
   - One or two sentences: what this paper adds because of that contrast.
   - Tie back to the paper's governing claim.

5. Optional: boundary citations.
   - Brief pointers to adjacent work you are not challenging, to avoid false fights.

## Paragraph Recipe (per cluster)

1. Topic sentence names the cluster's shared claim or approach.
2. Two to four sentences of shared ground (what is settled).
3. One sentence of limit or gap relative to your paper.
4. Bridge: how your design or theory addresses that limit.

## Practical Compression

When the section must be short:

1. Two or three nearest clusters only.
2. Shared ground + contrast in the same paragraph.
3. Close with the wedge sentence that restates your contribution against those neighbors.

## Editing Checks

- No citation dump: if a sentence is only a name list, regroup by claim.
- Do not strawman; steelman the best alternative, then contrast.
- Intro may preview positioning; the related-work section carries the catalog.
- Avoid coining a new jargon label just to stand apart.
- Every cluster should earn its space by changing what the reader thinks your paper must do.
