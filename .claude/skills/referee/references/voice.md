# Review voice

The review should read as if the user wrote it in one sitting: short, direct, a little blunt, and specific. Most of the user's corrections to past drafts said the same thing: too long, too padded, or it sounded like an AI wrote it.

## Length

- A major comment is a bold claim plus 2–5 sentences. A minor comment is 1–3 sentences.
- Use one or two telling examples, not an exhaustive list of every instance.
- If the user's note is a short question, the comment can be a short question.

## Never

- **Line numbers.** Don't write "(line 214)", "ll. 30–35", or "p. 12, line 4". Quote the phrase instead. Section, figure, table, and SI references are fine ("Section 3.4", "Table S6", "SI S3.3").
- **Parentheticals.** That covers asides, glosses, enumerations, and "e.g." lists in brackets. Rewrite them as their own sentence or cut them.
- **Detailed statistics the user didn't ask for,** such as CIs, df, and exact p-values, unless the point is about that number.
- **Periods in abbreviations,** if the project's past reviews leave them out ("eg", "vs", "pp"). Check the earlier reviews.

## AI tells to remove

- Gerund subjects. Not "Adding the control raises the effect" but "When the control is added, the effect grows."
- Abstract-noun subjects. Not "The question of which… is a good one" or "The lesson drawn from…" but a concrete subject and a plain verb.
- Stock phrases: "Indeed," "sits awkwardly," "lens," "drawing on," "becomes apparent," "amounts to," "rather than a downstream consequence," "it is worth noting," "importantly," "notably," "a key concern."
- Passive requests. Not "The resampling unit should be stated" but "How are the CIs computed?"
- Long chains joined by ", and …, so …". Break them into short sentences.
- Hedge stacks ("may potentially suggest"). Use one hedge, or none.

## Tone

- Write as a peer who read carefully and is puzzled or unconvinced, not as an authority handing down a verdict.
- Write "It's unclear what X means" and "I don't follow how Y follows from Z" in place of "The authors fail to…"
- Even a harsh review stays respectful. Give the benefit of the doubt in the phrasing, and let the substance carry the weight.
- Praise, when the notes contain it, is brief and specific.
