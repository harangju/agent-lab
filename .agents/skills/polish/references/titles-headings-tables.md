# Titles, Headings, and Tables Craft Guide (Universal Core)

Apply these universal rules alongside `../../draft/references/STYLE.md` and the matching paper-type playbook in `../../draft/references/genres/` (`ml.md`, `behavioral.md`, `general-science.md`, or `management-econ.md`).

## Paper Titles

1. **Front-load the key concept.** Put the core phenomenon, finding, or mechanism at the start of the title so scanners catch it immediately.
2. **Be precise, not cute or bloated.** Drop filler lead-ins (`"An Investigation of..."`, `"Towards..."`, unnecessary `"How..."`) and forced metaphors unless a short two-part `Concept: Concrete Claim` title genuinely sharpens the paper.
3. **Check cross-field term collisions.** Verify whether a key term carries a narrower or different meaning in the target venue's community, and ensure the title reads cleanly to your intended audience.
4. **Differentiate from sibling/prior papers.** If the team has a recent accepted or submitted paper on a closely related topic, verify the title clearly marks the new contribution.

## Section & Subsection Headings

1. **Match venue conventions.** Check target-venue exemplars and the matching `genres/` playbook before inventing custom section titles.
2. **Frame headings positively.** Describe what the section tests or establishes rather than opening negatively or defensively.
3. **Right-size granularity.** Group findings into clean thematic sections rather than over-fragmenting into many tiny subsections, and keep sibling headings grammatically parallel.

## Tables

1. **Every table must earn its space.** Do not duplicate a main-text figure verbatim; use tables for multi-column specifications, exact cell matrices, or comparisons that figures cannot carry cleanly.
2. **Hold comparisons consistent and honest.** Keep baseline definitions, sample scope, and evaluation conditions transparent across rows and columns—never silently change comparison sets without an explicit note.
3. **Zero cryptic shorthand.** Spell out column and row headers in plain English rather than raw script keys or opaque abbreviations.
4. **Use native venue typography.** Follow the venue's native font size, clean horizontal rules (`booktabs`), and readable spacing; avoid shrinking wide tables to illegibility with brute-force scaling hacks.
5. **Exact provenance.** Every cell must match locked `results/` outputs (and ideally be emitted directly by a committed script).
